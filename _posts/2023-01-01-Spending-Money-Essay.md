Over the years, people have been buying more and more products we might not need. For example, in 1900, we spent $1.5 trillion buying stuff. In 1975, we spent $12 trillion. That went up to $24 trillion in 1998 and $30.5 trillion in 2006. People buy so much because of a cycle of shopping making us crave items we don't need. When we buy something to enjoy like a toy or nice clothes, we feel happy. But after some time, the excitement of that something new goes away as we get used to it. With so many companies marketing their products to us, buying something new seems like the perfect solution to make us happy again. Over-consumption stops us from buying things we really need and spending money wisely. Let's consider that for a while and that next time we're shopping.
