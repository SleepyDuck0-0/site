This vacation brought me to three big cities: New York, Philadelphia, and Washington, D.C. During the trip, I slowly got familiar with living in an urban environment. Then arose the question of whether I would consider living in a big city when I grow up. This question has stuck in the back of my mind, and I thought of some points while I explored.

First, in the spirit of the "bad news first" tradition, here are the negative points. One given trait of a big city is the population. The sheer clutter and confusion of everybody trying to go to different places can make navigation difficult. The cities also had quite a few strange people and a noticeable risk of crime. These aspects made living in the city stressful, as you always had to be on the lookout. Also, something I noticed, especially in New York, was the trash. Taking a walk in the city was not as pleasant as taking a walk uptown.

The prospect of a city life has many pull factors, too. I think most people move to a city because of opportunity. So many people with ideas and needs means that there will almost always be someone who wants what you can do. An environment like that is a great place to be an entrepreneur. You can't make it big if you're presenting a product to a tiny town. This business opportunity idea works both ways. There is always someone who fits your desires, and there are higher-quality products in a big city.

In the end, this vacation turned out to be much more than I predicted. Instead of just being an experience now in the past, it presented a potential path for the future.
