Tig

Questions and Notes:

I really like how Peter turns out to be an imaginary friend. Everything before that starts to make sense, like how Peter never seemed to directly interact with other people. Still, I never would have seen that coming. He's even in the cover, so you would assume he is real.

I thought Tig’s real name would come as a surprise at the end when she's discovered herself and made a new family.

This book has one of the most unique writing styles I’ve seen. Every time it cuts to Tig’s old life, it is relevant to the current situation, like a memory you reflect on and it slowly reveals more and more about Tig. I really love how the protagonist’s thoughts were delivered.

Characters:

Tig:
She has a lot of emotion inside, but doesn't show them. She is relatively social, quickly becoming friends with Jacob and Jonah. But her trauma makes it hard for her to trust adults. Kids are very fast paced and think about many things at a time. So it was interesting to see a book from the kids’ perspective.
